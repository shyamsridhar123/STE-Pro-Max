# Qualified style checklist

Default: relaxed STE-inspired writing. Use these as review prompts, not automatic
rewrites. Preserve meaning, uncertainty, quotations, and technical identifiers.
The checker measures only approximate lengths; it cannot certify the standard.

## Apply without damaging the explanation

In the relaxed profile, improve a long sentence when it helps—not to meet an
arbitrary score. Keep articles, demonstratives, consistent terminology, and useful
vertical lists. Preserve an official technical name rather than mechanically
shortening it. Preserve necessary chronology and qualifications over tense targets.

Never ban a suffix, replace vocabulary globally, or invent an actor to remove
passive voice. Flag safety text for qualified review; do not infer hazards, reorder
safety-critical content blindly, or change risk categories merely for style.

The post's attached infographic is not the dictionary. For example, Issue 9
approves “approximately,” while “test” is noun-only (dictionary pages 166 and 401).
Consult the standard instead of turning the infographic into substitutions.

For an explicitly formal ASD-STE100 deliverable, distinguish the assistance this
tool provides from the independent vocabulary, counting, domain, and safety review
still required. Do not label the result certified or “80% compliant.”
